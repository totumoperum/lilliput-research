Privacy and data

Privacy.

A family-history enquiry may involve people who are living and who have not contacted us themselves. We want to explain what information may be involved, where it comes from and how to ask questions about it.

Who is responsible?

Research practice

The researcher responsible for independently initiated Lilliput Research enquiries is Rupert Murphy, operating as Lilliput Research, c/o The Lilliput Press, 62–63 Sitric Road, Arbour Hill, Dublin 7, D07 AE27, Ireland. The publisher is not the data controller for our research or correspondence.

Why records are researched

We investigate family histories, possible heirs and assets that may be unclaimed or unknown to relatives. Research aims to verify identity and relationships, assemble documentary evidence, establish an appropriate route for an enquiry and, where justified, tell a possible beneficiary about it. A research lead is not proof of legal entitlement.

Information involved

Depending on the case, information may include names, name variants, family relationships, dates and places of life events, addresses or historical residences, occupations, public contact routes, links to public profiles and correspondence about the matter. Some records also refer to deceased relatives, probate or other estates. We do not need your financial account credentials to make an initial enquiry.

Sources

Sources can include civil and parish registers, census and house or household registers, genealogical trees, newspapers, obituaries, cemetery records, family-history material, local archives, probate and court records, government notices, public directories, and information an individual or their authorised adviser supplies. A collaborative tree or public profile is treated as a research lead, not automatically as proof of identity or kinship.

Lawful basis and transparency

Independently initiated heir research may involve a legitimate-interest assessment under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR: identifying possible beneficiaries, documenting a family relationship and informing them of a potential claim, weighed against the rights of the people concerned. The legal basis and necessity of each type of processing must be assessed in context. Any commissioned research may involve other lawful bases or different controller–processor roles. These assessments are being finalised and should be explained as appropriate when a person is contacted.

Who may receive information

Relevant evidence may need to be provided to the person concerned, their instructed solicitor or estate representative, a bank, competent authority or records custodian as part of a lawful enquiry. Research also uses external archive and genealogy websites, research services, cloud storage, document digitisation, OCR and AI-assisted tools. Some providers may process information in jurisdictions outside the European Economic Area. The provider list, agreements and international-transfer safeguards are being reviewed; we cannot yet give a complete provider-by-provider schedule.

Research records and retention

Working materials include source references, research notes, family-tree hypotheses and documents, including conflicting or negative findings so that an unsupported connection is not repeated as fact. The practice also holds limited case-linked contact details in its research systems. No personal heir database is published on this website. Information should not be retained longer than necessary for an active case or a legitimate records obligation; a formal retention schedule, including inactive and unsuccessful leads, is still being determined.

Your rights

Where the GDPR applies, you may be able to request access, correction, erasure or restriction, or object to certain processing, subject to legal conditions. You can ask where particular information came from and say that you do not want further contact. Write to Rupert Murphy at the postal address above or use the reply details on your letter. The Irish Data Protection Commission provides independent guidance and handles complaints.

The website itself

This is a static website hosted through GitHub Pages. It has no enquiry form, first-party analytics or advertising tracker. Hosting may generate technical and security logs, and Google Fonts may receive a request from your browser for typefaces. The public pages do not list prospective heirs or contain private case files.
