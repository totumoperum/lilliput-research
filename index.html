Historical research

Documents before conclusions.

Family names travel through languages, borders, marriages and generations. A promising surname is a beginning, not proof of a relationship.

We combine civil and parish registers, probate papers, historical newspapers, cemetery records and published family histories. Each connection is checked against its source, with gaps and contradictions kept visible.

Areas of work

  • Reconstructing families and identifying likely living branches
  • Tracing potential next of kin named in unclaimed-estate notices
  • Locating original records that can prove or disprove a connection
  • Preparing documented genealogical findings for independent professional review

Research findings do not establish legal entitlement. Courts, estate administrators, banks and other relevant bodies determine eligibility under their own rules.

How a case progresses

From a notice to a documented family line

The process begins with an identifiable source, not a speculative promise of inheritance.

  1. An official notice is identified

    For example, an intestate estate, a public call for heirs or a dormant-asset notice.

  2. Records are compared

    Names, dates, places and family relationships are tested against original or attributable archival evidence.

  3. A potential relative may be approached

    We explain the research enquiry without asking for a payment or banking credentials in the first letter.

  4. The recipient chooses what to do

    Any proposed research or success-based fee is explained in writing. Independent legal advice is welcome.

  5. Formal claims are independently assessed

    Where appropriate, documentary findings can be taken to the relevant authority or a qualified legal professional.

Fees & independence

Clear terms, before any agreement.

Where Lilliput initiates an enquiry into unclaimed assets, we do not seek an advance payment from a person contacted as a potential relative. Any proposed fee is disclosed in writing, subject to the rules of the relevant jurisdiction.

Separately commissioned historical or genealogical work is quoted individually.

Our boundaries

  • No release charges, tax requests or bank-detail requests in an initial letter
  • No guarantee of inheritance or claim success
  • No legal advice or representation by Lilliput Research
  • Independent verification of both the notice and the researcher is encouraged

Received a letter?

How to check an enquiry is genuine

If you receive an unexpected letter about a family connection, treat it as a research enquiry rather than proof that money is owed to you. A credible approach should be verifiable independently.

  • Keep the letter reference and check the named researcher using a separately located contact route.
  • Ask what public notice or official record prompted the investigation.
  • Never send banking credentials or money to release an alleged inheritance.
  • Take independent legal advice before agreeing to any representation or fee.
