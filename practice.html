03 / Practice

The practice.

I'm an independent historical and genealogical researcher in Ireland. Much of my work involves finding an old notice, working out who the people named in it were, and putting together the family records that might explain it.

Approach

Putting the documents together.

The slightly curious part, often, is how neatly the paper trail joins up. A name on a register takes me to a will; that will names children or grandchildren; a later document may confirm where one part of the family went.

Other times the names or dates don't agree. I keep those contradictions in the research rather than making the family tree fit. I would rather say that a connection is still unproved than tell somebody they have an inheritance I cannot substantiate.

Process

  1. Find the noticeI look through published unclaimed-asset notices and probate records to find a starting point.
  2. Get the probate papersWhere relevant, I obtain official copies and check who the wills actually name.
  3. Follow the relativesA later will or civil record may independently confirm a relationship, or show that I have the wrong family.
  4. Find out what's neededBefore running up the cost of certificates, I try to establish what evidence the bank, administrator or authority would require.

Professional boundaries

I don't handle your money.

Although I can help with the documentary side, I recommend having an independent or family lawyer handle any claim that requires one. I won't ask to hold your money or to see your bank details. Any recovered funds should go directly to the relevant family member, estate or solicitor, not through me.

You can also pursue a claim without me. I'm happy to explain what records I've found and what further documents may be needed. I don't submit a claim or act for anyone without their agreement, and genealogical research alone cannot settle legal entitlement.
