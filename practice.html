03 / Practice

The practice.

Lilliput Research is a small independent practice founded and run by Rupert Murphy, an archaeologist and archival researcher. It is based in Dublin 7, above The Lilliput Press, and is entirely separate from the publishing business.

The founder

Rupert Murphy.

I came to genealogical work with a background in practical archaeology. In fieldwork, the importance of a find depends on its context; something similar is true of a name in a register. Its meaning comes from the household, the date, the place and the other records around it.

Much of my time is spent reading original material, obtaining probate papers, following variations of a name and reconstructing a family one generation at a time. Sometimes the documents join up rather neatly. At other times a missing certificate or a stubborn contradiction is precisely where the enquiry has to stop. I prefer to leave the uncertainty visible rather than supply a plausible fiction.

Process

  1. Begin with the surviving recordA register, family paper, probate file or local newspaper may supply the first reliable fact.
  2. Follow the generationsCompare the names, places and relationships with civil, parish and household records.
  3. Set out the evidencePrepare a family tree with original source references, keeping ambiguities and gaps in view.
  4. Inform and assistWhere appropriate, bring a possible entitlement to a relative's attention and identify the documents an institution may require.

Professional boundaries

The family's claim, not ours.

Lilliput Research prepares genealogical evidence; it is not a bank, probate court or law firm. Any claim should be made by the person legally entitled or a representative they appoint. We do not ask to receive or hold recovered funds, and clients are welcome to use their own solicitor.

Possible relatives are under no obligation to engage us. They may approach the relevant institution independently. A family tree and supporting papers can help establish a relationship, but legal ownership, the identity of an account holder and the allocation of an asset must be determined by the appropriate institution or authority.
