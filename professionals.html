For solicitors, executors and administrators

Documentary genealogy.

Where the family relationships in an estate remain uncertain, we undertake original-record research and reconstruct the relevant lines of descent. The findings are presented with their sources, so a solicitor or administrator can examine the evidence rather than rely on an unsupported pedigree.

Research work

From a name to a documented family.

A single identity may have to be examined in several forms: the name used at birth, the married name in a local paper, the address in a household register and the person named in a later probate document. We compare these records within their historical context, drawing where necessary on parish and civil registers, newspaper archives, local histories and original testamentary papers.

Research may concern a particular individual, a missing generation or a family line extending overseas. Reports distinguish relationships supported by records from those which remain merely possible, and identify any material gaps for further enquiry.

Possible deliverables

  • A documented family-tree chart showing relevant generations and the underlying record references
  • Source references and copies of records where reproduction or disclosure is permitted
  • A chronology of births, marriages, deaths and probate events
  • Outstanding proof requirements, inconsistencies and rejected identity matches
  • A clearly scoped research summary suitable for professional review

We do not provide regulated legal advice, certify legal ownership, act as a court-appointed administrator or receive estate funds. Scope, fees, source-access restrictions and any permitted handling of personal data should be agreed before work begins.
